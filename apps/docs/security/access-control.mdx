---
title: "Access control and workspace security"
description: "Design least-privilege roles, contextual access, MFA, and audit reviews for recruiting teams."
---

Harly uses workspace-scoped RBAC. A role combines module permissions with an
optional context such as assigned jobs, departments, or regions.

## Role design

Start with job responsibilities, not with a copy of your organisation chart:

| Role | Typical access |
| --- | --- |
| Owner | Workspace, billing, security, integrations, and all recruiting data |
| Hiring manager | Assigned jobs, applications, interviews, and feedback |
| Recruiter | Candidate and application workflow for allowed jobs or regions |
| Interviewer | Assigned interviews and scorecards |
| Coordinator | Scheduling and candidate communications without role administration |

The exact permission set depends on your workspace policy. Keep administrative
permissions separate from candidate-data permissions.

## Contextual scopes

Use `assigned` jobs when a member should work only on their requisitions. Add
department or region constraints for larger teams. The server resolves the
active workspace and applies the role policy before returning candidates,
applications, interviews, or offers.

<Warning>
  A scoped administrator cannot create a role broader than its own privilege
  ceiling. Review role changes in the audit log after every organisational
  change.
</Warning>

## MFA and session controls

Owners can enforce MFA from **Settings → Security**. For higher-risk teams,
also configure passkeys, trusted domains, IP allowlists, and risk detection
according to the deployment environment.

## Audit review

Review security and administrative events monthly and after an incident. Export
the audit log before deleting or anonymising records required for an active
investigation. A candidate's deletion request is not a substitute for an
incident record.
